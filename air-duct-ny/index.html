    Duct Leakage in New York, NY Homes: What Measurement Reveals


    Duct leakage is not uniformly distributed. The most common leak locations in New York, NY residential duct systems are the register boot connections where the duct meets the ceiling or floor surface, the takeoff connections at the main plenum where individual branch runs connect to the trunk, and the mid-run connections where duct sections join at flex fittings or sheet metal collars. These connections were sealed at installation with mastic or tape that has cracked, dried, or separated over years of thermal cycling.

    A duct blaster test at a New York, NY property measures total leakage across all these points simultaneously: a calibrated fan pressurizes the system to 25 Pascals and the flow rate required to maintain that pressure is the CFM25 leakage value. We then calculate leakage per 100 square feet of conditioned floor area to normalize the measurement. A New York, NY home with a measured leakage rate of 18 CFM25 per 100 square feet has twice the leakage of the 8 to 10 target for existing homes, which translates directly into the energy and comfort gap the homeowner has been experiencing.

    After sealing, the same duct blaster measurement confirms the improvement with a specific number rather than a general assurance. The before-and-after comparison is the documentation that separates a verifiable result from a service performed and assumed to have helped.

    What the Leakage Rate Means for Your New York, NY Home's Heating and Cooling Cost


    The relationship between duct leakage and energy cost is mechanical. Every CFM of conditioned air that escapes into an unconditioned space like an attic or crawlspace is air the HVAC system had already expended energy to condition. The system then runs an additional cycle to make up the temperature deficit caused by that loss. In a New York, NY home with a high cooling load in summer, that additional runtime accumulates across every cooling cycle through the hottest months.

    Sealing the ducts does not improve the efficiency of the HVAC equipment. It delivers what the equipment already produces to the rooms it was designed to serve rather than to the spaces above the ceiling and below the floor.

    Dryer Vent Service Across New York, NY


    CMR Air Duct Cleaning serves New York, NY homeowners with professional dryer vent cleaning and installation assessment. The IRC M1502 requirements for dryer vent configuration apply across New York, NY jurisdictions, and many existing homes have installations that do not meet those requirements regardless of how long they have been in place.

    The most common non-compliant configurations we find in New York, NY homes: dryer vents terminating in the attic rather than the building exterior, flexible plastic or ribbed foil duct used for the main duct run (prohibited as a fire risk due to lint accumulation in the corrugations), and screens installed at the exterior termination cap (explicitly prohibited because lint blocks them rapidly). These configurations are common because many appliance installers focus on connecting the dryer rather than verifying code compliance of the full exhaust system.

    We clean the full duct run, assess the installation against IRC M1502 requirements, note any configurations that fall outside those requirements, and provide a scope for correction if indicated. The cleaning itself addresses debris accumulated in the duct. The assessment tells you whether the installation configuration is creating conditions for that debris to accumulate faster than it should.

    Structural Duct Repair in New York, NY


    Physical duct failures in New York, NY homes follow predictable patterns based on duct location and age. Flex duct in attics has a rated service life of 15 to 25 years under normal conditions. Attic heat in New York, NY summers accelerates the degradation of flex duct outer insulation and inner liner material. Flex duct in New York, NY attics that is approaching or past its rated lifespan often shows visible degradation: compressed or torn outer insulation, brittle inner liner material that splits at connection points, and connections that have lost the mastic seal that held them at installation.

    Disconnected joints are the most common structural failure we find in New York, NY attic inspections. A joint that has separated allows the full volume of conditioned air intended for the rooms downstream to escape into the attic. The room loses its cooling or heating entirely, and the system works harder because it cannot reach the setpoint even with extended runtime.

    We assess the structural condition of New York, NY duct systems as part of every repair and cleaning visit, identify the specific failures present, and provide both spot repair and replacement options when the extent of damage makes both relevant.

    Frequently Asked Questions


    • How does CMR Air Duct Cleaning's approach to duct service in New York, NY differ from a general HVAC company? General HVAC companies in New York, NY focus on the mechanical performance of the air handler and outdoor unit: refrigerant levels, electrical components, and system cycling. Ductwork is often outside the scope of a standard HVAC service call unless a dramatic failure has occurred. CMR specializes in the duct system specifically: we measure leakage before and after sealing, assess dryer vent installations against code requirements, inspect flex duct structural condition in attics and crawlspaces, and repair physical failures rather than just diagnosing them. The measurement tools we use for duct leakage are not standard equipment on HVAC service vehicles.
    • How often do New York, NY homes actually have significant duct leakage? In our experience across New York, NY properties, homes with duct systems older than ten to fifteen years very commonly have leakage rates above the 8 to 10 CFM25 per 100 square foot target for existing homes. Boot connections, plenum takeoffs, and mid-run joints that were sealed with tape at original installation often show tape failure by this point. Homes that have had any attic work (insulation upgrades, pest control, roofing) may have additional damage from foot traffic on flex duct runs that were not adequately supported or protected during the work.
    • What New York, NY building code requirements apply to dryer vent installation? New York, NY follows the International Residential Code, which specifies in Section M1502 that dryer exhaust ducts must be smooth rigid metal of at least 4-inch diameter, must terminate to the building exterior (never in an attic, crawlspace, or garage), must be equipped with a backdraft damper at the termination point, must not have screens at the termination, and must not exceed the calculated maximum equivalent length for the run. Specific local amendments may apply in your New York, NY jurisdiction. We assess your existing installation against the IRC baseline and note any configurations that fall outside those requirements.
    • Does CMR Air Duct Cleaning service ductwork in both attics and crawlspaces in New York, NY? Yes. Ductwork in unconditioned attics and crawlspaces presents specific performance and condition challenges that differ from ducts inside conditioned living spaces. Attic ductwork is subject to extreme temperature differentials that accelerate flex duct material degradation and increase heat gain into supply ducts. Crawlspace ductwork is subject to moisture exposure that can damage insulation, create biological growth conditions, and cause physical degradation of connection seals. We inspect and service duct systems in both locations across New York, NY.
